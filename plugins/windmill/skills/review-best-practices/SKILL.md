# Performance Review Best Practices

## Specificity
- Ground every claim in concrete behavior or output: what the person did, in what situation, with what result.
- Avoid vague superlatives ("great communicator") without an example.
- Reference specific projects, milestones, or incidents when you can.

## Evidence
- Use only evidence available through the review context loaded by the tool (self-review text, peer feedback, upward feedback, prior notes).
- Do not invent examples or borrow details from other employees.
- When evidence is thin, say so rather than overstating.

## Fairness and Bias Avoidance
- Apply the same evaluative bar across all reviewees. Do not adjust expectations based on tenure, background, or identity.
- Watch for recency bias (weighting only the last few weeks) and halo/horn effects (letting one strong or weak signal dominate).
- If peer or upward feedback conflicts, acknowledge the range rather than flattening it.

## Tone
- Developmental, not punitive: describe what happened and what better looks like.
- Direct and honest: softening critical feedback to the point of obscuring it is not kindness.
- Calibrate length to question importance; do not pad or truncate.

## Relational Element
- For managers writing about a report: frame feedback around how you will help them grow, not just what they did.
- For participants completing their own self-review or peer feedback: an honest, grounded self-assessment or peer assessment is more useful to the person than flattery.
- When helping someone think through their own review, ask clarifying questions to surface concrete examples before drafting.

## When to Defer to the Human
- Before submitting or triggering any outward-facing action (submit, share, release), preview what will happen and ask for confirmation.
- If the user seems uncertain about what to write, offer to talk through the questions first rather than generating a draft immediately.
- Submitted answers are not easily reversed; a submitted manager review isn't accessible to the receiving employee until it is shared with them, but submission itself is a meaningful commit. Confirm intent before calling the tool.
- When in doubt about whether an action is reversible, surface the question rather than proceeding.
